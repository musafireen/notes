# Sadducees

Politically, they seem aligned with the priestly elite and Temple authorities 
[[bbl.acts#c4#1]]

Acts 4:1–2 — Sadducees oppose Peter and John for preaching the resurrection of Jesus.
[[bbl.acts#c4#2]]

Many high priests and members of the Sanhedrin were Sadducees (cf. Acts 5:17).

The high priest (a Sadducee) and his sect arrest the apostles.
[[bbl.acts#c5#17]]

Acts 23:6–8 — Paul exploits the Pharisee-Sadducee divide:
[[bbl.acts#c23#6]]

Matthew 22:23–34 / Mark 12:18–27 / Luke 20:27–40 — Sadducees test Jesus with the resurrection question:

They deny resurrection and the afterlife.

They use a hypothetical about levirate marriage to challenge Jesus.

Jesus refutes them, saying they misunderstand the Scriptures.
[[bbl.evngl#mark#c12#18]]
[[bbl.evngl#luke#c20#34]]

Matthew 3:7 — John the Baptist rebukes both Pharisees and Sadducees:

“But when he saw many of the Pharisees and Sadducees coming to where he was baptizing, he said to them: ‘You brood of vipers! Who warned you to flee from the coming wrath?’”
[[bbl.evngl#matthew#c3#7]]

Matthew 16:1–12 — Sadducees and Pharisees demand a sign from Jesus; Jesus warns his disciples about their “leaven” (teaching).
[[bbl.evngl#matthew#c16#1]]

# pharisee

The earliest non-biblical source is Josephus (Antiquities 13.10.5, ~93 CE), but he describes events from the Hasmonean period (~150 BCE):

“The Pharisees have delivered to the people a great many observances by succession from their fathers, which are not written in the Law of Moses; and for that reason the Sadducees reject them…”

[[jsphs#jud#b13#10.5]]

This matches Mark 7:3–8, showing the NT authors reflect Pharisaic practice known in Josephus’ time.

Mark 7:3–8 — Detailed note on Pharisaic traditions:

“For the Pharisees and all the Jews do not eat unless they wash their hands properly, holding to the tradition of the elders…”
This is one of the rare places where their ritual practices are explicitly described.
[[bbl.evngl#mark#c7#3]]

Mark 8:11 — They demand a sign from heaven:

“The Pharisees came and began to argue with him, seeking from him a sign from heaven to test him.”
[[bbl.evngl#mark#c8#11]]

Mark 2:16 — They criticize Jesus for eating with tax collectors and sinners:

“When the scribes of the Pharisees saw that he was eating with sinners and tax collectors, they said to his disciples, ‘Why does he eat with tax collectors and sinners?’”
[[bbl.evngl#mark#c2#16]]

Mark 2:24 — Dispute about Sabbath observance:

“The Pharisees said to him, ‘Look, why are they doing what is not lawful on the Sabbath?’”
[[bbl.evngl#mark#c2#24]]

Matthew 5:20 — Jesus contrasts their righteousness:

“Unless your righteousness exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven.”
[[bbl.evngl#matthew#c5#20]]

Matthew 9:11 — Similar to Mark 2:16 but stronger.
[[bbl.evngl#matthew#c9#11]]

Matthew 12:2 — Sabbath dispute, same as Mark but more accusatory.
[[bbl.evngl#matthew#c12#2]]

Matthew 23 — The “Seven Woes” against the Pharisees:

“Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites!” (repeated multiple times).
This chapter presents the most detailed critique of Pharisaic teachings and practices in the NT.
[[bbl.evngl#matthew#c23]]

Luke 7:36 — A Pharisee invites Jesus to dine with him.
[[bbl.evngl#luke#c7#36]]

Luke 13:31 — Some Pharisees warn Jesus about Herod’s plan to kill him — a rare instance showing them in a positive or at least neutral light.
[[bbl.evngl#luke#c13#31]]

Luke 18:10–14 — The parable of the Pharisee and the tax collector illustrates self-righteousness.
[[bbl.evngl#luke#c18#9]]-14

John 3:1–2 — Nicodemus, a Pharisee, comes to Jesus by night.
[[bbl.john#apcl#c3#1]]

John 7:47–49 — Pharisees dismiss the crowds as ignorant.
[[bbl.john#apcl#c7#47]]

John 9:13–16 — Pharisees question Jesus about healing on the Sabbath.
[[bbl.john#apcl#c9#13]]

Acts 23:6 — Paul declares himself a Pharisee, exploiting their doctrinal differences with the Sadducees:

“Brothers, I am a Pharisee, a son of Pharisees. It is with respect to the hope and the resurrection of the dead that I am on trial.”
[[bbl.acts#c23#6]]
