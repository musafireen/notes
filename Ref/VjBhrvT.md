## 41
śaṃbhavopāya is linked to desire or will as the sudden upsurge of emotion and instinct that shatters thought construction, thereby enabling the adept to perceive the nonduality of consciousness. This can be achieved through extreme situations of fear or through inducing pain by scratching the arm with a sharp instrument (VB 93), through the arising of sexual desire (VB 41, 73) and so on

तन्त्र्यादिवाद्यशब्देषु दीर्घेषु क्रमसंस्थितेः।
अनन्यचेताः प्रत्यन्ते परव्योमवपुर्भवेत्॥४१॥

He who gives undivided thought (ananya-cetāḥ) to the sounds of musical instruments (such as) the chorded ones, etc. (tantrī-ādi-vādya.śabdeṣu) —which --i.e. the sounds-- are long (dīrgheṣu) due to the continuity of the succession (krama-saṁsthiteḥ)— becomes (bhavet) at the end --viz. when such sounds have ceased and no other support emerges-- (pratyante) the form of the Supreme Ether (para-vyoma-vapus)||41||
## 73
śaṃbhavopāya is linked to desire or will as the sudden upsurge of emotion and instinct that shatters thought construction, thereby enabling the adept to perceive the nonduality of consciousness. This can be achieved through extreme situations of fear or through inducing pain by scratching the arm with a sharp instrument (VB 93), through the arising of sexual desire (VB 41, 73) and so on

गीतादिविषयास्वादासमसौख्यैकतात्मनः।
योगिनस्तन्मयत्वेन

The yogi who is one with the incomparable bliss because of the enjoyment in the spheres of songs, etc. (gīta-ādi-viṣaya-āsvāda-asama-saukhya-ekatā-ātmanaḥ... yoginaḥ),  his identification with it --that is, "with the incomparable bliss"-- (tad-mayatvena), 

मनोरूढेस्तदात्मता॥७३॥

is identification with that (Supreme Brahma) (tad-ātmatā) through the development or expansion of the mind (manas-rūḍheḥ)||73||
## 93
śaṃbhavopāya is linked to desire or will as the sudden upsurge of emotion and instinct that shatters thought construction, thereby enabling the adept to perceive the nonduality of consciousness. This can be achieved through extreme situations of fear or through inducing pain by scratching the arm with a sharp instrument (VB 93), through the arising of sexual desire (VB 41, 73) and so on

किञ्चिदङ्गं विभिद्यादौ तीक्ष्णसूच्यादिना ततः।

After piercing (vibhidya) at the beginning (ādau) any limb --"fingers, thumb, etc." according to Kṣemarāja and Śivopādhyāya-- (kiñcid aṅgam) with a pointed needle, etc. (tīkṣṇa-sūci-ādinā), (and,) after that (tatas), 

तत्रैव चेतनां युक्त्वा भैरवे निर्मला गतिः॥९३॥

after directing or fixing (yuktvā) consciousness (cetanām) right there (tatra eva), (there occurs) a clean arrival (gatiḥ) (nirmalā) at Bhairava (bhairave) --Kṣemarāja and Śivopādhyāya wrote "bhairavaviṣayaṁ nirmalaṁ jñānaṁ bhavatyeva" or "the pure Knowledge relating to Bhairava takes place"--||93||

# 104
śāktopāya, in contrast to the śāmbhava that is without thought construction ( nirvikalpa), is achieved by using the faculty of cognition. By focusing on a pure thought construction ( śuddhavikalpa) that corresponds to a true state of affairs, such as “I am Śiva” or “I am omnipresent” (VB 104), the mind is gradually purified until the truth of the claim is existentially realized (on conceptions of mind see Flood 2021)

विहाय निजदेहास्थां 

After abandoning (vihāya) (all) consideration for his own body (nija-deha-āsthām), 

सर्वत्रास्मीति भावयन्।

the one who contemplates (bhāvayan) “I am (asmi) everywhere (sarvatra... iti)” 

दृढेन मनसा दृष्ट्या नान्येक्षिण्या सुखी भवेत्॥१०४॥

with a steady mind (dṛḍhena manasā) (and) with a vision that perceives nothing else (dṛṣṭyā na anya-īkṣiṇyā), becomes (bhavet) happy (sukhī)||104||
