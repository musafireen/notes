Metrum dactylicum hexametrum inuentum primitus ab Orpheo Critias adserit, 

Critias asserts that the dactylic hexameter was invented originally by Orpheus,1 

Democritus a Musaeo, 

Democritus by Musaeus.

Persinus a Lino, 

Persinus by Linus; 

permulti ab Homero, 

and very many by Homer. 

qui profecto, cum heroum facta carmine suo explicare uellet, 

Homer, indeed, when he wanted to explain the deeds of heroes in his poetry, 

metri huius, quod ceteris omnibus longe pulchrius celsiusque est, aut repertor aut certe adprobator fuit. 

was either the inventor or at least the approver of this meter, which is by far more beautiful and nobler than all others.

Constat autem metrum dactylicum hexametrum heroicum ex dactylo et spondio uel trochaeo, 

The heroic dactylic hexameter meter consists of a dactyl and a spondee or a trochee, 

ita ut recipiat spondium locis omnibus, 

in such a way that it receives a spondee in all positions, 

dactylum locis omnibus praeter ultimum, 

a dactyl in all positions except the last, 

trochaeum uero loco tantum ultimo. 

and a trochee only in the final position.

Huius exemplum  

An example of this

p. 590 vol. VI Keil

‘‛arma uirumque cano, Troiae qui primus ab oris’. ’

I sing of arms and the man, who first from the shores of Troy.
