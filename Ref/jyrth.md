#### 258
अथ व्रतादीनां चरणाचरणं व्याचष्टे –

Now, the practice (caraṇa) and the non-practice (acaraṇa) of ascetic vows and spiritual disciplines (vratādīnām) appears.

व्रतचर्या च मन्त्रार्थतादात्म्यप्रतिपत्तये ॥ ४-२५८ ॥

The performance of ascetic vows (vrata-caryā ca) is meant for achieving direct identity with the absolute meaning and reality of the mantra (mantra-artha-tādātmya-pratipattaye). 
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.258]]

तन्निषेधस्तु मन्त्रार्थसार्वात्म्यप्रतिपत्तये ।

Conversely, the absolute prohibition or abandonment of those very vows (tan-niṣedhas-tu) is meant for achieving the realization that the absolute reality of the mantra is all-encompassing and identical with the entire universe (mantra-artha-sārvātmya-pratipattaye).
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.259 ]]

च शब्दात् सिद्धान्ते उक्ता, – इत्यनुवर्तनीयम् “मन्त्रार्थो नियतो वाच्यदेवतादिः, 

By virtue of the word 'and' (ca-śabdāt), the phrase 'as declared in the orthodox Śaiva Siddhānta' must be understood as an ellipsis. In that context, 'the meaning of the mantra' (mantra-artho) refers to a specific, restricted deity to be addressed, along with its matching ritual parameters (niyato vācya-devatā-diḥ).

“तन्निषेधः” अर्थात्कौले ।
शार्वात्म्यम्” विश्वाभेदः ।

The phrase 'the prohibition or abandonment of those vows' (tan-niṣedhaḥ) applies, in reality, to the transgressive Kaula path (kaule). The term 'all-encompassing nature' (sārvātmyam) means absolute, non-dual identity with the entire universe (viśva-abhedaḥ).

इह पुनस्तस्या न विधिर्निषेधो वा, – इत्येतत् सर्वं

However, within this supreme method [of Śāktopāya], there is neither a binding injection to perform these vows (vidhiḥ) nor a binding prohibition to abandon them (niṣedho vā). 

पूर्वमेव व्याख्यातम्, इति न पुनरायस्तम् ॥ २५८ ॥

Because all of this has already been thoroughly explained previously, no further effort is made to elaborate here. [This concludes the comment on Verse 258.]
#### 259
अथ क्षेत्रादिसम्प्रवेशं व्याख्यातुमाह –

Now, in order to explain the spiritual entry (sam-praveśam) into sacred fields (kṣetra) and surrounding holy sites, the author declares: 

क्षेत्रपीठोपपीठेषु प्रवेशो विघ्नशान्तये ॥ ४-२५९ ॥

'The intentional entry into the sacred fields (kṣetra), seats of power (pīṭha), and secondary power centers (upa-pīṭheṣu) is executed solely for the absolute pacification and erasure of all spiritual obstacles (vighna-śāntaye).'
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.259]]

मन्त्राद्याराधकस्याथ तल्लाभायोपदिश्यते ।

Now, [this method of entering sacred sites] is instructed for the sake of attaining That [the ultimate fruits or visual presence of the deity] for the worshiper of the mantras and other divine forces (mantra-ādi-ārādhakasya-atha tal-lābhāya-upadiśyate).
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.260]]

“क्षेत्रम्” मेलापस्थानम्, “पीठम्” कामरूपादि “उपपीठम्” देवीकोट्टादि ।

The term 'Field' (kṣetram) means the actual physical location of spiritual meeting and gathering (melāpa-sthānam). The term 'Seat of Power' (pīṭham) refers to places such as Kāmarūpa and others. The term 'Secondary Seat' (upapīṭham) refers to places such as Devīkoṭṭa and others.

“तल्लाभाय” इति तस्य मन्त्रादेः प्रियमेलापादिक्रमेण सिद्धादेर्लाभः, तन्निमित्तं वा ।

The compound phrase 'for the sake of attaining That' (tal-lābhāya) means gaining the direct presence, blessing, and attainment of the Siddhas and advanced masters (siddha-ādeḥ lābhaḥ). This occurs through the sequential progression of the deeply beloved, mystical gathering (priya-melāpa-ādi-krameṇa) hosted by the deity of the mantra or it serves as the instrumental cause for that very event (tan-nimittaṃ vā).
,

“क्षेत्रोपक्षेत्रसन्दोहाद्याश्रयान्निर्मलो भवेत् ॥
"

इति ॥ २५९ ॥

As it has been declared [in the authoritative scriptures]: 'By taking refuge in the massive collections of sacred fields (kṣetra) and secondary fields (upa-kṣetra), a practitioner becomes entirely stainless, pure, and free from all limitations (nirmalo bhavet).' #### 260
अन्यत्र चात्र निषेधः कृतः – इत्याह –  

Elsewhere, the prohibition of this [external practice] is made. Thus, the author says:

क्षेत्रादिगमनाभावविधिस्तु स्वात्मनस्तथा ॥ ४-२६० ॥  

The injunction against going to holy places and the like is for oneself [to know] in that manner,
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.260]]

वैश्वरूप्येण पूर्णत्वं ज्ञातुमित्यपि वर्णितम् ।  

to know the fullness [of reality] through its all-pervasive nature. This has also been described. || 4-260 ||
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.261]]

आदिशब्दात् पीठादेर्ग्रहणम् ।  

By the word "and the like" (_ādi_), places of worship like Pithas and others are included.

“तथा” इति प्रागुक्तेन प्रकारेण ।  

The word "thus" (_tathā_) refers to the manner previously stated.

तदुक्तम् –  

As it is said:

नातः किञ्चिदपास्यं प्रक्षेप्तव्यं च नात्र किञ्चिदपि ।  

Nothing is to be cast away from this, and nothing is to be added to it.

परिपूर्णे सत्यात्मनि किं नु क्षेत्रादिपर्यटनैः ॥  

When the Self is completely full, what is the use of wandering to holy places and the like?

इति ।  

[End of quote].

इह पुनरेतदुभयमपि नास्तीति प्रागेवोक्तम्, इत्याह – “इत्यपि वर्णितम्” इति ।  

It has already been stated that in this state, neither of these two exists. Thus, the author says: "This has also been described."

तदुक्तम् –  

As it was said:

“इह सर्वात्मकेकस्मात्तद्विधिप्रतिषेधने ।  

"In this state, because everything is the single Self, both injunctions and prohibitions [cease to apply]."

" (४।२५७) इति ॥ २६० ॥  

(4.257) [End of quote] || 260 ||
#### 261
अथ समयादिप्रपालनमाचष्टे –  

Now, the author explains the observance of the rules of conduct (Samayas) and the like:

समयाचारसद्भावः पाल्यत्वेनोपदिश्यते ॥ ४-२६१ ॥  

The true essence of the rules of conduct and customary practices is taught as something to be strictly observed. || 4-261 ||
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.261]]
#### 262
![[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.262]]

निर्मर्यादं स्वसम्बोधं सम्पूर्णं बुद्ध्यतामिति ।  

[This is done] so that the boundless, completely full self-awareness may be realized.
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.263]]

इदं कुर्यादिदं न कुर्यात्, – इत्येवमात्मा समयाचारः ।  

"One should do this, and one should not do that"—such is the nature of the rules of conduct (Samayachara).

“भेदप्राणतया” इति किञ्चित् हि त्यक्त्वा किञ्चिदुपादीयते इत्येवमात्मा भेदः, यथा शास्त्रान्तरत्यागेन स्वशास्त्रे प्ररोहः ।  

By the phrase "because duality is their very lifebreath," it means that duality consists of abandoning something and accepting something else, just as one firmly takes root in one's own scripture by abandoning other scriptures.

यद्वक्ष्यति –  

As the author will state later:

“अन्यस्तमन्त्रो नासीत सेव्यं शास्त्रान्तरं च नो ।  

"One should not sit with those who have received initiation into other mantras, nor should one resort to other scriptures.

अप्ररूढं हि विज्ञानं कम्पेतेतरभावनात् ॥  

For spiritual knowledge that has not yet taken deep root wavers due to the contemplation of other systems."

" (२५।५६३) इति ।  

(25.563) [End of quote].

समविषमलक्षणेषु “मतशास्त्रेषु” पुनः शमयादेर्निषेधो” विहितः ।  

However, in the dualistic scriptures (_Mata-shastras_) which deal with characteristics of the equal and unequal, the prohibition of rules of conduct and the like is prescribed.

तथा च तत्रत्यो ग्रन्थः इत्याह – निर्मर्यादमित्यादि” ।  

And so, referring to the text found there, the author says: "boundless...", and so forth.

तद्धि परं तत्त्वं श्वसम्बोधम्” स्वप्रकाशम्, अत एवानन्यापेक्षत्वात् शम्पूर्णम्,” अत एव चानियत रूपत्वात् निर्मर्यादम्” निर्यन्त्रणं “बुध्यताम्” अनुभूयतामित्यर्थः ।  

For that supreme reality is "self-awareness" (meaning self-luminous); therefore, because it depends on nothing else, it is "completely full"; and therefore, because its form is unrestricted, it is "boundless" (meaning free from constraints); the meaning is "let it be realized/experienced."

तदेवम् एवंविधे परे तत्त्वे कथं नाम हानोपादानाद्यपेक्षासहस्रसम्भिन्नः समयाचारः शुद्धिनिमित्तम्, इति भावः ॥ २६२ ॥  

Therefore, in this kind of supreme reality, how can the rules of conduct—which are shattered by thousands of expectations of abandonment and acceptance—possibly be the cause of purification? This is the underlying intent. || 262 ||
#### 263
अथ “परस्वरूपलिङ्गादि नामगोत्रादिकं च यत्” इति व्याख्यातुकामः क्रमेण परकीयं स्वकीयं च रूपाद्याचष्टे –  

Now, wishing to explain [the passage] "that which is the form, emblem, etc., of the other, as well as the name, lineage (gotra), etc.," the author describes in sequence the form and other traits belonging to the other and to oneself:

परकीयमिदं रूपं ध्येयमेतत्तु मे निजम् ॥ ४-२६३ ॥  

"This form belongs to the other and is an object of meditation, but this [other] is my own inherent form."
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.263]]

ज्वालादिलिङ्गं चान्यस्य कपालादि तु मे निजम् ।  

"And the emblem made of flames and the like belongs to the other, but the skull and other emblems belong inherently to me."
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.264]]

“एतत्” इति ध्यातृस्वभावम् ।  

The word "this" (_etat_) refers to the very nature of the meditator.

“ज्वालादि” इति बाह्यैषणादिसमुत्थत्वात् परकीयम् ।  

The phrase "flames and the like" (_jvālā-ādi_) belongs to the other because it arises from external desires and external objects.

निजम्” इति स्वशरीरावस्थितम् ॥ २६३ ॥  

The word "inherent/my own" (_nijam_) refers to that which resides within one's own body. || 263 ||
#### 264
“लिङ्गादि” इत्यादिशब्दार्थमाह –  

In the phrase "emblem and the like" (_liṅgādi_), the author explains the meaning of the word "and the like" (_ādi_):

आदिशब्दात्तपश्चर्यावेलातिथ्यादि कथ्यते ॥ ४-२६४ ॥  

By the word "and the like," ascetic penance, ritual conduct, proper timing, lunar days, and so forth are expressed. || 4-264 ||
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.264]]

“तपः” चान्द्रायणादि, “चर्या” चर्यापादोक्त आचारः, “वेला” मध्याह्नार्धरात्रादिरूपा, “तिथिः” प्रतिपदारूपा ॥ २६४ ॥  

"Ascetic penance" (_tapaḥ_) refers to practices like the Chandrayana fast; "ritual conduct" (_caryā_) refers to the code of conduct taught in the ritual sections (Charyapada) of the scriptures; "proper timing" (_velā_) takes the form of midday, midnight, and so forth; "lunar day" (_tithiḥ_) takes the form of the first day of the lunar fortnight (pratipat) and others. || 264 ||
#### 265
नाम शक्तिशिवाद्यन्तमेतस्य मम नान्यथा ।  

"The name of this one ends with 'Shakti', 'Shiva', and so forth; [so is] mine, not otherwise."
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.265]]

“एतस्य” सैद्धान्तिकस्य साधकस्य, व्रतपरिग्रहादौ पुष्पपातादिक्रमेण क्रियमाणं नाम शक्तिशिवाद्यन्तं स्यात् ।  

"Of this one" means of the practitioner belonging to the Siddhanta school, whose name given during the acceptance of vows through the ritual of dropping a flower and so forth, should end with 'Shakti', 'Shiva', and the like.

तेन शिखाशक्तिः, ईशानशिवः; आदिशब्दाद्गणाद्यन्तम्, यथा कवचगणः ।  

Therefore, examples are Shikhashakti and Ishanashiva; by the word "and so forth" (_ādi_), names ending with 'Gana' and the like are included, such as Kavachagana.

तदुक्तम् –  

As it is said:

श्रजं विमोचयेन्नाम दीक्षितानां तदादिकम् ।  

"One should release the garland [to determine] the name and so forth of the initiates.

शिवान्तकं द्विजेन्द्राणामितरेषां गणान्तकम् ॥  

It should end in 'Shiva' for the best of the twice-born (Brahmins), and end in 'Gana' for the others."

" इति ।  

[End of quote].

तथा  

Likewise:

“शिशुना क्षिप्तमकामान्निपतेद्यत्र नाम तत्पूर्वम् ।  

"Wherever the garland thrown unintentionally by a child falls, that [deity's name] becomes the prefix of the name.

शक्त्यन्तं नारीणां शिवशब्दान्तं नृणां कुर्यात् ॥  

One should make the names of women end with 'Shakti', and the names of men end with the word 'Shiva'.

एवं विप्रक्षत्रियविशां शूद्राणां तु भवेद्गणप्रान्तम् ॥  

Thus it is for Brahmins, Kshatriyas, and Vaishyas; but for Shudras, the name should end with 'Gana'."

" इति ।  

[End of quote].

“अन्यथा” इति बोध्याद्यन्तम्, स्वस्वसन्ततिक्रमेणौवल्ल्यन्तं हि पूजा नाम भवेत्, – इति रहस्यशास्त्रविदः, तदुक्तम् –  

The word "otherwise" (_anyathā_) refers to names ending with 'Bodhi' and the like; for according to the experts of the esoteric scriptures (_rahasya-shāstra_), the ritual name should end with 'Avali' according to the lineage of each traditional line. As it is said:

“बोधिः प्रभुस्तथा योगी आनन्दः पाद आवलिः ।  

"Bodhi, Prabhu, likewise Yogi, Ananda, Pada, and Avali—

वीराणां वीरपत्नीनां कल्प्यं नामैतदन्तकम् ॥  

the name for the Heroes (Veeras) and the wives of Heroes should be designed ending with these terms."

" इति ।  

[End of quote].

तेन सत्यबोधिः विश्वप्रभुरित्यादि ॥  

Therefore, examples are Satyabodhi, Vishvaprabhu, and so forth.

गोत्रं च गुरुसन्तानो मठिकाकुलशब्दितः ॥ ४-२६५ ॥  

And the lineage (_gotra_) is the lineage of the Guru, which is designated by the terms 'Mathika' and 'Kula'. || 4-265 ||
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.265]]

तस्य मठिकेति कुलमिति चाभिधानद्वयम् ॥ २६५ ॥  

That [lineage] has two names: 'Mathika' and 'Kula'. || 265 ||
#### 266
तत्र का मठिका ? – इत्याह –  

Regarding that, what is a Mathika (monastic lineage)? Thus, the author says:

![[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.266]]

युगक्रमेण कूर्माद्या मीनान्ता सिद्धसन्ततिः ।  

In the sequence of the world ages (Yugas), the lineage of the Siddhas begins with Kurma and ends with Mina.
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.267]]

न केवलमर्धचतस्र एव मठिका यावदन्या अपीत्याह – “युगेत्यादि” ।  

It is not that there are only three and a half Mathikas, but there are others as well; thus, the author says: "In the sequence of the world ages...", and so forth.

आद्यशब्दस्तन्त्रेण व्याख्येयः; तेन कूर्मस्य त्रेतायुगावतारकस्य श्रीकूर्मनाथस्याद्यः कृतयुगावतारकः श्रीखगेन्द्रनाथः स आद्यो यस्याः सा तथेति ।  

The word "beginning with" (_ādya_) should be explained using the rule of tantra (simultaneous application); therefore, the one who comes before Kurma—the incarnation in the Treta Yuga, Shri Kurmanatha—is Shri Khagendranatha, the incarnation in the Krita Yuga; thus, it means that lineage of which he (Khagendranatha) is the first.

कुलशब्दस्य गुरुकुलमित्यादौ लोकप्रसिद्धेः पृथग्व्याख्यानं कृतम् ॥ २६६ ॥  

Because the word 'Kula' is already well-known in the world in expressions like 'Gurukula' (the teacher's household), a separate explanation of it has not been made. || 266 ||

“गोत्रादि” इत्यादिशब्दार्थमाह –

आदिशब्देन च घरं पल्ली पीठोपपीठकम् ॥ ४-२६७ ॥  

And by the word "and the like," the lineage subgroup (_ghara_), the village settlement (_palli_), and the primary and secondary sacred seats (_pīṭha_ and _upapīṭha_) are expressed. || 4-267 ||

#### 268
मुद्रा छुम्मेति तेषां च विधानं स्वपरस्थितम् ।  

"The seal (_mudrā_) and the secret gathering-sign (_chummā_), and their ritual application based on one's own or another's tradition."
[[abhNvGp#tntrlok#4.268]]

“घरम्” इति षण्णां साधिकाराणां राजपुत्राणां भिन्नं भिन्नमाश्रमस्थानम् ।  

The word "house" (_gharam_) refers to the distinct hermitage locations of the six authoritative spiritual princes (the direct disciples of Matsyendranatha).

“पल्ली” भिक्षास्थानम् ।  

The word "settlement" (_palli_) refers to the designated place for gathering alms.

यद्वक्ष्यति –  

As the author will state later:

“एते हि साधिकाराः पूज्या येषामियं बहुविभेदा ।  

"For these are the authoritative ones, worthy of worship, whose lineage is highly diversified,

सन्ततिरनवच्छिन्ना चित्रा शिष्यप्रशिष्यमयी ॥  

unbroken, and multifaceted, consisting of disciples and grand-disciples.

आनन्दावलिबोधिप्रभुपादान्ताथ योगिशब्दान्ता ।  

The names ending with the words Ananda, Avali, Bodhi, Prabhu, Pada, and Yogi—

एता ओवल्ल्यः स्युर्मुद्राषट्कं क्रमात्त्वेतत् ॥  

these are the secondary lineages (_ovallis_); and the six seals (_mudras_) follow this sequence:

दक्षाङ्गुष्ठादिकनिष्ठान्तमथ सा कनीयसी वामात् ।

आवाहनानन्तरकर्तव्यमुपदेष्टुमाह–

In order to instruct the essential spiritual duty that must be executed immediately after the formal invocation (āvāhana) of the deities, the text declares [in Verse 51]:

आवाहिते मन्त्रगणे पुष्पासवनिवेदनैः ॥ २६-५१ ॥

First, the satiation/replenishment (tarpaṇaṃ) must be executed by means of sacrificial blood (raktaiḥ prāk); only afterwards (paścāt) should it be expanded through the detailed offerings of flowers, incense, and the rest (puṣpa-dhūpa-ādi-vistaraiḥ).

ननु आवाहनानन्तर्येण तर्पणमेव कार्यमित्यत्र कि प्रमाणम्?– इत्याशङ्क्याह–

Now, a sharp objection is raised: 'What indeed is the authoritative scriptural proof (kiṃ pramāṇam) for the rule that satiation/feeding (tarpaṇam) must be performed immediately after the formal invocation (āvāhana-ānantaryeṇa)?' Anticipating this doubt, the author declares [in Verse 53]:

If, however, one fails to perform the ritual satiation/feeding (na kuryāt tarpaṇaṃ yadi) of the living mantra-deity once it has arrived and manifested (āgatasya tu mantrasya)... [then a severe spiritual violation occurs].

शिवूजापरो मौनी मद्यमांसपराङ्मुखः ॥

इत्यादि लक्षणमुक्तम् ।

Now, a strong objection is raised: Everywhere in the common scriptures, without any distinction, the true characteristics of a devotee or worshiper of the Supreme Lord (bhagavad-ārādhakasya) are declared as follows: 'He must be free from pride or deceit (adāmbhikaḥ), completely devoted to the Guru (gurau bhaktaḥ), a strict celibate (brahmacārī), one who has conquered his senses (jitendriyaḥ), perpetually intent on the worship of Śiva (śiva-pūjā-paraḥ), observant of silence (maunī), and utterly averse to wine and meat (madya-māṃsa-parāṅmukhaḥ).' — Such are the rules universally stated.

तत्कथमिह बाह्यस्थया शक्त्या सह समर्चयेदित्युक्तम्?–इत्याशङ्कां परमेश्वरोक्त्यैव निरवकाशयन्नमुष्य विधेः पीठिकाबन्धं करोति–

That being the case, how can it be commanded here [in the Kaula ritual] that one must perform worship together with a female partner who stands outside conventional boundaries (bāhyasthayā śaktyā saha samarcayed-ity-uktam)? Leaving absolutely no room or scope (niravakāśayan) for this specific doubt, and doing so exclusively by citing the authoritative word of the Supreme Lord Himself (parameśvara-uktyā-eva), the author constructs the foundational framework (pīṭhikā-bandhaṃ karoti) of this esoteric ritual injunction:

उक्तं श्रीयोगसञ्चारे ब्रह्मचर्ये स्थितिं भजेत् ।

It has been declared in the sacred Śrī Yogasañcāra Tantra: 'The practitioner must firmly abide in the state of Celibacy (brahmacarye sthitiṃ bhajet).'

ननु ब्रह्मैव नाम किं यदाचरणेऽपि स्थितिं भजेत्?–इत्याशङ्क्य आह–

Now, an inner inquiry is raised: 'What indeed is the true meaning of the word "Brahman" [in Brahmacarya, the conduct of Brahman], such that by practicing it one is said to abide in the ultimate state?' Anticipating this question, the text declares [in Verse 97]:

आनन्दो ब्रह्म परमं तच्च देहे त्रिधा स्थितम् ॥ ९७ ॥

'Absolute Bliss (ānandaḥ) is the Supreme Brahman (brahma paramaṃ), and that [Bliss/Brahman] resides within the physical body (tac-ca dehe) in a threefold manner (tridhā sthitam).'
#### 98
उपकारि द्वयं तत्र फलमन्यत्तदात्मकम् ।

Two [of these elements] serve as the immediate helpers or instruments (upakāri dvayaṃ) within that triad, while the other element is the ultimate fruit (phalam-anyat) which is identical in nature to that [Supreme Bliss].

“आनन्दो ब्रह्मणो रूपं… ।
"

इति ।
परममित्यनेन अस्य अवश्यसेव्यत्वमुक्तम् ।

As it is declared: 'Bliss is the very form of Brahman...' By utilizing the word 'Supreme' (param-iti), the absolute necessity of engaging with and consuming this bliss (asya avaśya-sevyatvam) is directly decreed.

तच्च न केवलं परब्रह्मादिविभेदमात्मनि एव स्थितं यावदनात्मरूपे बाह्यशरीरादावपि–इत्याह–देहे इति ।

And that [Bliss] does not reside solely within the absolute Self (ātmani eva sthitaṃ), which is free from the divisions of the Supreme Brahman and so forth; rather, it extends all the way into that which is non-Self—namely, the external, physical body and its material components (anātma-rūpe bāhya-śarīra-ādāv-api). This is precisely why the text uses the word: 'within the body' (dehe iti).

तत्रेति–त्रयाणां मध्यात् ।
द्वयमिति–मद्यमांसलक्षणम् ।
अन्यदिति–मैथुनम् ।

The word 'therein' (tatra-iti) means out of the midst of the three bodily locations/elements. The word 'two' (dvayam-iti) directly designates the characteristics of wine (madya) and meat (māṃsa). The word 'the other' (anyad-iti) refers explicitly to sexual union (maithunam).

मद्यमांसपानाशनप्रवर्धितधातुर्हि रममाण आनन्दमियादित्युक्तम्– उपकारीति फलमिति च ।
अत एव तदात्मकमिति सर्वशेषत्वेन उक्तम् ।
तच्छब्देन च अत्र आनन्दपरामर्शः ॥

For it is declared that a practitioner whose physical tissue elements (dhātuḥ) have been highly energized and stimulated by the drinking of wine and the eating of meat (madya-māṃsa-pāna-aśana-pravardhita) attains a surging, unconditioned Bliss while engaging in sexual delight (ramamāṇaḥ ānandami-yād-ity-uktam).This is exactly why the text categorizes them using the terms 'the two helpers' (upakāri-iti) and 'the ultimate fruit' (phalam-iti). For this very reason, the phrase 'identical in nature to that' (tad-ātmakam-iti) is stated to show that it is the ultimate culmination of all practices (sarva-śeṣatvena uktam). Here, the word 'that' (tat-śabdena) serves as a direct reference back to Bliss (ānanda-parāmarśaḥ).

एवमेषां ब्रह्ममयत्वादेतदनुष्ठाता ब्रह्मचारीत्युच्यते–इत्याह–

In this manner, because these three transgressive substances are completely identical to the essence of Brahman (eṣāṃ brahma-mayatvāt), a practitioner who actively engages in their ritual execution is designated as a true celibate monk (brahmacārī-ity-ucyate). Thus, the text declares [in Verse 98]:

ओष्ठ्यान्त्यत्रितयासेवी ब्रह्मचारी स उच्यते ॥ ९८ ॥

'One who ritually consumes the triad that terminates in the final labial consonant (oṣṭhya-antya-tritaya-sevī) is truly called a Brahmacārī!'

#### 115
क्षुभ्नात्यनुचक्राण्यपि तानि 

At that time [of union], it fiercely agitates even the secondary, subordinate sensory wheels (anucakrāṇi-api); 

तदा तन्मयानि न पृथक्तु ।

yet those wheels then become completely identical with that [central bliss-consciousness] and are no longer separate (na pṛthak tu).

सङ्घट्टवेलायां हि ऊर्ध्वधामनि परानन्दमये योगिनीवक्त्रात्मनि मुख्यचक्रे समावेशतारतम्यात् जातः सम्यक् देहाद्यभिमानन्यग्भावेन क्षोभः पूर्णतालक्षणः स्वात्मचमत्कारातिशयो यस्य, 

For at the precise moment of mutual union (saṅghaṭṭa-velāyāṃ hi), within the highest station (ūrdhva-dhāmani) which is filled with supreme bliss (para-ānanda-maye) and is identical to the 'mouth of the Yogini' (yoginī-vaktra-ātmani)—which is the primary sovereign wheel (mukhya-cakre)—there is born an internal agitation or churning (kṣobhaḥ). This agitation is triggered through the progressive intensity of mystical absorption (samāveśa-tāratamyāt) which completely subordinates and dissolves the ordinary identification with the physical body and ego (samyak dehādi-abhimāna-nyagbhāvena). This churning is characterized solely by a state of absolute fullness (pūrṇatā-lakṣaṇaḥ), and it is the highest peak of the ecstatic wonder of one's own Self (sva-ātma-camatkāra-atiśayo).

एवंविधं तत् शक्तिशक्तिमल्लक्षणं युगलमनुचक्राण्यपि क्षुभ्नाति तदेकमयतयैव परामृशेत् ?–इत्यर्थः ॥

Such a divine couple (evaṃ-vidhaṃ tat yugalam), who embody the absolute union of Power and the Holder of Power (śakti-śaktimat-lakṣaṇaṃ), violently churns and agitates even all the subordinate wheels of the senses (anu-cakrāṇy-api kṣubhnāti). Should one then perceive or experience these senses as anything other than completely identical to that central core of bliss (tad-ekamayatayaiva parāmṛśet)? [No, they cannot be seen as separate.]—This is the exact meaning.

ननु देहाद्यभिमानन्यग्भावेन तत्र समाविष्टस्य क इव अनुचक्रार्थः?–इत्याशङ्क्य उक्तम्–

Now, an objection is raised: 'If a practitioner has entered into total absorption there by completely overriding their bodily identification, what could possibly be the function or meaning of the secondary sense-wheels (anu-cakra-arthaḥ) during this state?' Anticipating this doubt, the author states: 

तानि तदा तन्मयानि न पृथक् तु इति ।  

'At that peak moment, those secondary sense-wheels become entirely saturated with that central consciousness (tāni tadā tan-mayāni); they do not exist separately or as isolated entities (na pṛthak tu).

अथ च अत्र परस्पराहननालिङ्गनपरिचुम्बनादिलक्षणः क्षोभः ॥

Furthermore, in this specific context, the physical agitation or churning (kṣobhaḥ) is explicitly characterized by acts such as mutual striking/clashing (paraspara-āhanana), intense embracing (āliṅgana), and deep kissing (paricumbana), and so forth.

If it is asked: 'Does the dynamic awakening and sudden rise of the Supreme, unconditioned Consciousness (parasyā eva saṃvidaḥ samudayaḥ) take place exactly in this manner?' Anticipating this question, the text declares [in Verse 115]:'

इत्थं यामलमेतद्गलितभिदासङ्कथं यदेव स्यात् ॥ ११५ ॥

Thus, when this Divine Couple (yāmalam-etad) enters the state where even the slightest talk or trace of duality has completely melted away (galita-bhidā-saṅkathaṃ yad-eva syāt)... [then the Supreme reality shines forth].'
#### 116
क्रमतारतम्ययोगात्सैव हि संविद्विसर्गसङ्घट्टः ।  

Through the application of sequential intensity, that very same pure Consciousness transforms into the creative emission and the bliss of mutual union. 

तद्ध्रुवधामानुत्तरमुभयात्मकजगदुदारसानन्दम् ॥ ११६ ॥

That is the immutable station, the Absolute, which is identical to the universal joy overflowing with a universe comprised of both subject and object.
#### 117
नो शान्तं नाप्युदितं शान्तोदितसूतिकारणं परं कौलम् ।

The Supreme Kaula state (paraṃ kaulam) is neither quiescent (no śāntam) nor is it emergent (nāpy-uditam); rather, it is the ultimate cause that gives birth (sūti-kāraṇam) to both the quiescent and the emergent states.

विसर्गसङ्घट्ट इति–सङ्घट्टरूपो विसर्गः–इत्यर्थः ।  
उभयात्मकेति–शिवशक्तिसामरस्यमयजगदानन्दरूपम्–इत्यर्थः ।  

The phrase 'the creative emission of mutual union' (visarga-saṅghaṭṭaḥ) means, in reality, the creative emission which takes the exact form of absolute friction/collision (saṅghaṭṭa-rūpo).The phrase 'comprised of both' (ubhaya-ātmaka) means, in truth, the state of universal joy (jagadānanda) which is saturated with the perfect, undifferentiated fluid-oneness (sāmarasya) of Śiva and Śakti.

शान्तमिति–विश्वोत्तीर्णम् ।  
उदितमिति–विश्वमयम् ।  
परं कौलमिति– शान्तोदितादिशब्दव्यपदेश्यत्वायोगादतीव रहस्यरूपम् –इत्यर्थः ।  

The quiescent state' (śāntam) means that which is utterly transcendent to the universe (viśvottīrṇam).'The emergent state' (uditam) means that which is fully immanent within the universe (viśvamayam).'The Supreme Kaula state' (paraṃ kaulam) means that which possesses an exceedingly secret, mystical nature (atīva rahasya-rūpam) because it cannot be limited or designated (vyapadeśya-ayogāt) by dualistic words such as 'quiescent' or 'emergent'.

अथ च क्षेपस्य असम्पत्तेर्न शान्तं स्वस्वरूपविश्रान्त्या च न उदितं किन्तु एतदवस्थाद्वयहेतुभूतमनवच्छिन्नसंविन्मात्रसतत्त्वम्– इत्यर्थः ॥

Furthermore, it is 'not quiescent' because the outward projection of objects has not ceased; and it is 'not emergent' because it rests firmly in its own essential nature (sva-svarūpa-viśrāntyā). Rather, it is the unconditioned, absolute essence of pure Consciousness alone (anavachinna-saṃvin-mātra-satattvam), which serves as the fundamental cause (hetu-bhūtam) for both of these states.

एतदावेशे च अवश्यमवधातव्यम्–इत्याह–

And regarding immersion (āveśe) into this state, one must absolutely maintain unbroken attention—as the text declares [in Verse 117]:

अनवच्छिन्नपदेप्सुस्तां संविदमात्मसात्सदा कुर्यात् ॥ ११७ ॥

'One who desires to attain this unconditioned, limitless station (anavachinna-pada-īpsuḥ) must perpetually internalize and absorb (ātmasāt sadā kuryāt) that supreme, dynamic Consciousness.
